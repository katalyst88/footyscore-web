What is on it

Six screens, and none of them is a list of everything at once.

The round

Every match as a card: both clubs, the goals and behinds, the total, and a line carrying the quarter and the clock. Matches in play sort to the top.

One match

The headline score, the breakdown, the score by quarter with the current quarter marked, and the leading goalkicker for each side.

Goal by goal

Every score in reverse order — who kicked it, which quarter, the time on the clock, and the score after it went through.

The ladder

Position, club, record and premiership points, sorted on points and then percentage, with the finals cut drawn as a real line.

The finals bracket

Worked out from the ladder rather than fetched. Who plays who — and where the rule does not settle a spot yet, it says so instead of guessing.

A tile and complications

One swipe from the watch face: your club as the hero with three more matches beside it. Or the score on the face you already wear.

Alerts

Three settings, not one switch.

Everything is off until you turn it on. Then goals, quarter breaks and the final siren are three separate settings, and each one is off, your club, or every match.

So you can take every goal in the round and still only hear about your own club at quarter time. One buzz fifteen minutes before your club plays, with no score in it. And one game can be muted on its own without touching any of it.

While your club is playing the score is checked at most once a minute. Between matches nothing is checked at all, and off the charger below 20 per cent it slows down further.

Watching it later

Nothing gives it away.

Hide every score — on the tile, on the watch face and in the app — until you open a match yourself. The goals and behinds go with it, because 12.9 is 81 to anyone who follows football.

The alerts switch off at the same time, since every one of them is the result arriving early.

What it costs

$10, once

Free to download, and everything works for fourteen days. After that one payment keeps it for good. No subscription. No advertising.

The fourteen days are days football is actually played, not days on the calendar. Install it in December and the fourteen days are still waiting for you in March.

What it does not do

  • No account, and nothing to sign in to.
  • No advertising, and no third-party analytics.
  • No club logos — they are trademarks, and eighteen of them would be eighteen network fetches on a watch.
  • No phone app required, and nothing to pair.
  • Scores come from third-party sources. They are unofficial, and they can be late or wrong.
